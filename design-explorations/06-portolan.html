Plot the course before you sail —
every slide is a working chart.

A portolan is the mariner's working chart: courses plotted by bearing before departure, never by feel. That is the deck's argument in one object. The surface is harbor-day light — sage paper with a faint rhumb net radiating beneath — and everything floats over it as rounded white cards. One kelp green carries every action and every emphasis. Human gates become harbors: you make port, get cleared, then sail the next leg. Each chart closes with an edition pill — number, chapter, corrected-to date — because a chart that doesn't carry its date gets ships lost.

Best for
Bright conference rooms. The only light direction of the six — calm, print-like, credible, and legible from the back row.
Costs you
Soft contrast wants a projector check. In a dark room the five dark directions will out-punch it.
Risk taken
A full nautical metaphor — navigation, harbors, bearings — carried by warmth and roundness instead of technical severity.
