Legible from
the back row.

A solid chapter-coloured panel bleeds off the left edge of every slide, carrying a knocked-out numeral and the chapter name. Content sits on bone-white beside it. No radius, no shadow, no gradient — flat colour and heavy uppercase display type do all the work. Where two things are being compared, the answer is literally blocked in solid colour so the room sees the point before it reads the table.

Best for
A large room, a projector with poor contrast, or a talk you want people to remember the shape of.
Costs you
The panel eats a quarter of every slide. Content has to be cut hard — which is arguably the point.
Risk taken
Display type set enormous and set in caps. Loud on purpose; the least "corporate" of the five.
